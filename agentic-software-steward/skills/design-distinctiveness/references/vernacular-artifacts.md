# Vernacular Artifacts

Collect evidence of the business's lived identity before choosing a direction. Look beyond the current website; its expression may be the thing being replaced.

## Build The Inventory

Capture what the business has actually made, used, said, or earned:

- Physical identity: signage, storefront, vehicles, uniforms, interior, equipment, packaging, and materials.
- Working documents: invoices, estimates, work orders, intake forms, menus, programs, schedules, and price boards.
- People: owner and staff names, faces, roles, founding story, and people customers repeatedly mention.
- Customer language: recurring review phrases, what customers thank the business for, and local names for products or services.
- Local texture: street, plaza, neighborhood, route, season cycle, service territory, and community events.
- Heritage objects: an invented product, award, wall art, mascot, tool, ritual, recipe, or mark the business treats as its own.
- Conversion reality: the actual booking, registration, quote, carryout, visit, or contact path customers use today.

For each item record:

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Artifact | The observed object, person, phrase, behavior, or proof. |
| Source | Exact business-owned page, official profile, client material, public review, local press, or observed place. |
| Authority | `business-owned`, `client-supplied`, `customer-stated`, `third-party`, or `open`. |
| Currentness | `current`, `dated`, `conflicting`, or `unknown`. |
| Use status | `expression-ready`, `inspiration-only`, `client-confirmation`, or `rights-review`. |
| Design consequence | What decision this evidence may inform. |

Use public reviews and third-party sources as evidence about language and reputation, not as authority for current operating facts. When business-owned sources disagree, record the conflict instead of silently selecting the most convenient answer.

## Synthesize, Do Not Cosplay

- Let the strongest relevant artifacts inform the creative thesis; do not require a literal hero treatment.
- Let paperwork, signage, environment, and spoken language inform typography and rhythm without turning the interface into a costume.
- Use customer language to sharpen vocabulary, not to fabricate testimonials.
- Treat a real owner or staff member as a candidate design asset when the evidence shows that person is central to trust; follow consent and image-use boundaries before publication.
- Preserve the real conversion path or intentionally improve it; do not let visual invention delete it.
- Abstract, combine, exaggerate, or contrast evidence when doing so strengthens the audience experience.

The creative thesis, page physics, typographic voice, and signature expression must each trace to evidence or a stated audience-serving contrast. Do not demand a citation for every spacing or color adjustment.

## Boundaries

- Follow `../../visual-direction/references/image-use-and-truth.md` for ownership, provenance, documentary truth, and reuse.
- Follow `../../brand-direction/references/anti-anchoring.md` for the blank-page pass. Existing website composition, section order, copy, and agency styling remain disposable unless approved.
- Mark unverified or conflicting operating details for resolution. Do not invent supporting detail around them.
- Keep source notes and rights status in internal review artifacts, never as customer-facing badges or caveats.

## Failure Tells

- A rationale made only of mood words or an invented metaphor.
- Literal artifact cosplay that substitutes novelty for audience fit.
- A concept with no real names, objects, customer language, or conversion reality.
- A beautiful page that removes the business's existing route to booking, buying, registering, visiting, or requesting service.
- A claim stripped despite a clear business-owned source, or a conflicting fact presented as settled.

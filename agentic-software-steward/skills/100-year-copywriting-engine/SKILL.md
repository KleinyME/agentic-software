# 100 Year Copywriting Engine

Apply Ken Moskowitz's advertising experience as strategic judgment, not as a formula dispenser. Start with the buyer, the offer, the proof, and the action. Use a framework only when it helps those facts land.

## Operating Contract

- Preserve the project's approved brand voice and product truth.
- Never invent a metric, testimonial, customer, deadline, guarantee, certification, comparison, or product capability.
- Treat source examples as calibration, never as facts or reusable phrases.
- Keep private planning, prompt instructions, framework labels, claim flags, and review commentary out of public copy.
- Never send a raw model response directly to a UI renderer, CMS publish action, ad account, or email send.
- Use named copywriters as sources of techniques and history, not as undisclosed voices to imitate. Use Ken's supplied voice guide only when the user explicitly requests Ken's voice or the approved project voice calls for it.

## Start With Existing Truth

Before asking questions, read available product and brand context such as:

- `PRODUCT.md`;
- `docs/brand/voice.md`;
- approved brand or creative direction;
- customer interviews, reviews, support language, or sales notes;
- verified proof, offer details, pricing, and current product behavior.

For client-site work, treat an approved `brand-direction` brief, reference-site delta, desired feeling, and primary customer action as a complete creative brief. Do not restart a long interview.

Ask at most one or two questions at a time, and only when the answer would change the copy strategy. Do not run a fixed interview when the repository or brief already answers the question.

Establish the smallest sufficient brief:

1. Artifact and channel.
2. Intended audience and awareness stage.
3. One primary action.
4. Product, offer, and meaningful alternative.
5. Verified proof and unresolved truth dependencies.
6. Desired brand behavior, including how bold or playful the copy may be.

When foundational marketing context is missing, route through `brand-copy-steward` rather than improvising positioning.

## Preview Creative Posture

For a client-review preview, create the strongest coherent positioning, promise, headline, and offer supported by the direction. Push beyond the inferred comfort zone when boldness is part of the brief. Keep provisional claim status in `CLIENT_REVIEW.md`, never as hedging, warnings, or annotations inside the rendered page. Before production, route claims through `brand-copy-steward` and deployment readiness for confirmation, evidence, implementation, revision, or removal.

## Choose Strategy Before Framework

Write a one-sentence strategy:

```text
For [audience in situation], make [offer/outcome] feel [credible desired belief] so they [one action], using [proof or mechanism].
```

Then select the lightest useful framework from [references/frameworks.md](references/frameworks.md):

- AIDA for a general persuasion path.
- PAS for a real, already-felt problem. Do not manufacture anxiety.
- PASTOR for high-consideration offers that need story, proof, and detail.
- BAB for a concrete before/after transformation.
- 4 Us for headline exploration, without inventing urgency or specificity.
- QUEST or SCQA for educational and B2B persuasion.
- APP for supported competitive positioning.
- StoryBrand for customer-as-hero message clarity.
- H.E.L.P.F.U.L for substantive educational content; read [references/helpful-blog-framework.md](references/helpful-blog-framework.md).

The framework is hidden structure. Do not announce it in the published copy, force every step into a visible section, or preserve it when a more natural structure works better.

## Set Voice Without Pastiche

Describe voice on behavioral axes: energy, warmth, authority, density, humor, pressure, and evidence. Use [references/writer-styles.md](references/writer-styles.md) as a technique library. Do not automatically select or imitate a living writer's recognizable style.

When Ken's voice is explicitly requested, read [references/kens-voice.md](references/kens-voice.md). Preserve its useful qualities: fresh situational humor, confident clarity, vivid comparisons, and conversational expertise. Do not copy phrases or invent personal experiences.

## Create The Copy

When direction is unsettled, create two or three genuinely different concepts. Vary the promise, angle, or proof strategy, not only adjectives. Recommend one and explain the choice outside the public copy. When direction is approved, produce one focused draft rather than unnecessary alternatives.

For every draft:

- lead with a recognizable customer situation, tension, desire, or outcome;
- make one central promise;
- connect features to consequences the customer cares about;
- use exact customer language when available;
- earn specificity with source facts;
- make the next action obvious and proportionate;
- use humor, urgency, scarcity, and power words only when true and on-brand.

Read [references/power-words.md](references/power-words.md) only for headline or CTA exploration. Read [references/super-bowl-ad-bible.md](references/super-bowl-ad-bible.md) for original video or cinematic concept development; use only abstract creative principles.

## Protect Claims And Stage Truth

Classify material claims outside the public artifact as `supported`, `positioning`, `proof_needed`, or `placeholder`. For concept work, keep persuasive copy strong while putting unresolved proof in the designated review artifact. Before production, prove it, build it, adjust it without losing the idea, or remove it.

Never turn an illustrative example such as a made-up percentage, time saving, result, testimonial, or price into public copy.

## Product UI Rule

Ordinary product UI is not a direct-response ad. For buttons, labels, onboarding, empty states, confirmations, errors, and permissions, prioritize the user's task, next action, and recovery path. Keep tone consistent but reduce theatrical hooks, agitation, urgency, and persuasion. Route the final public surface through `brand-copy-steward` and `audience-boundary`.

## Output Contract

Separate customer-facing copy from review notes, implementation mapping, and test hypotheses. Only the clean public copy may enter the renderer or publishing path. Never embed private fields in HTML data attributes, client hydration state, metadata, or hidden components.

If the user asks for paste-ready copy, lead with clean public copy. Put rationale, alternatives, and review notes in visibly separate sections.

## Editorial Passes

Before delivery, review strategy, truth, value, voice, flow, action, audience boundary, and human quality. Use [references/quality-standards.md](references/quality-standards.md) as an effects-based review, not a universal word blacklist. Use [references/optimization.md](references/optimization.md) to turn alternatives into measurable hypotheses. Verify current platform limits before using [references/platform-specs.md](references/platform-specs.md). Finish with `ai-writing-audit` without bleaching the voice.

## Completion Standard

Copy is ready only when it is strategically coherent, recognizably on-brand, supported by available truth, free of internal residue, correctly separated for its audience, and suitable for the actual channel. A framework-shaped draft is not finished merely because every framework step appears.

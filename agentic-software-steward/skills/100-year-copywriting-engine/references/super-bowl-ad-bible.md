# Super Bowl Ad Bible — Iconic Ad Creative DNA

Source: Super Bowl Ad Bible — Ken "Spanky" Moskowitz / Ad Zombies

This file contains complete AD_KNOWLEDGE_PACKAGE entries for 27 iconic ads. Each package includes Master Creative DNA, Transferable Creative Principles (safe to use without copying IP), Scene Structure Map, Camera/Lens/Lighting Map, and Safety Abstraction rules.

**Use this file when:** Creating video scripts, TV/broadcast copy, cinematic ad concepts, or any content drawing on iconic ad creative principles.

**Safety note:** All "TRANSFERABLE CREATIVE PRINCIPLES" and "ALLOWED" sections are cleared for use. Never replicate the exact IP elements listed in "DO NOT" sections.

## AD_KNOWLEDGE_PACKAGE: APPLE_1984

**TITLE:** "1984"
**BRAND:** Apple | **YEAR:** 1984 | **DIRECTOR:** Ridley Scott
**CATEGORY:** Cinematic · Dystopian · Manifesto
**TONE:** Oppressive → Chaotic Break → Hopeful
**NARRATIVE DEVICE:** Lone rebel disrupts authoritarian conformity
**WHY ICONIC:** Radically redefined Apple's identity; cinematic world-building; metaphor for liberation through personal computing.

### MASTER CREATIVE DNA
**Core Themes:** Conformity vs individuality | Oppression vs liberation | Technology as empowerment | Heroic disruption of the status quo

**Visual DNA:** Industrial dystopia | Smoke, haze, hard light | Blue/gray color palette | Brutalist architecture | Worker uniformity as background texture

**Audio DNA:** Mechanical hum | Footstep rhythms | Authoritarian broadcast tone | Crescendo during rebellion | Stillness in final VO

**Human Performance DNA:** Workers: emotionless, robotic movement | Runner: determination, breath, athletic energy | Guards: frantic, aggressive pursuit | Shift must be visible: oppression (rigid) → disruption (chaotic) → release (softening)

### TRANSFERABLE CREATIVE PRINCIPLES
1. Build a rigid, oppressive world with a singular controlling force.
2. Insert a lone human character who disrupts that rigidity.
3. Use a kinetic moment (object throw, bold act) to break the tension.
4. Contrast cold oppressive light with warm liberated light.
5. End with a quiet emotional resolution after the chaos.
6. Camera language must evolve: Act 1 controlled/symmetrical → Act 2 chaotic/handheld → Act 3 slow/serene.

### SCENE STRUCTURE MAP
- **Act 1 — OPPRESSION:** Calm, cold, mechanical
- **Act 2 — DISRUPTION:** Chaotic, kinetic, handheld
- **Act 3 — LIBERATION:** Warm, hopeful, slow

**Pacing Curve:** 0–3s oppressive stillness | 3–6s kinetic escalation | 6–9s impact moment | 9–12s emotional release | 12–15s philosophical VO resolution

### CAMERA/LENS/LIGHTING MAP
- Act 1 Lenses: 24mm/35mm (wide, oppressive scale)
- Act 2 Lenses: 50mm handheld / 35mm chaos / 85mm slow motion
- Act 3 Lenses: 24mm wide serenity
- Color Temp: Oppression 3800–4200K | Heroic disruption 4500–5000K | Liberation 5000–5200K
- Movement: Act 1 slow/symmetrical | Act 2 handheld/erratic | Act 3 slow dolly-out

### SAFETY ABSTRACTION
**DO NOT replicate:** Apple-owned imagery, Big Brother likeness, specific dialogue, characters, or designs.
**ALLOWED:** Tonal structure | Cinematography principles | Emotional arc | Pacing | Thematic contrast | Color language | Symbolic disruption

## AD_KNOWLEDGE_PACKAGE: NIKE_JUST_DO_IT_WALT_STACK

**TITLE:** "Just Do It — Walt Stack"
**BRAND:** Nike | **YEAR:** 1988 | **DIRECTOR:** Joe Pytka
**CATEGORY:** Documentary · Authentic · Inspirational Minimalism
**TONE:** Grounded · Genuine · Understated Humor
**NARRATIVE DEVICE:** Real man jogging while offering simple, matter-of-fact wisdom.
**WHY ICONIC:** Established the "Just Do It" ethos through authentic human storytelling rather than spectacle.

### MASTER CREATIVE DNA
**Core Themes:** Endurance through simplicity | Age is irrelevant; action matters | Stoic positivity | Authentic lived experience | Ordinary life as inspiration

**Visual DNA:** Natural light only | Zero production fuss | Slow, steady camera | Urban everyday environment | Emphasis on gait, breath, skin texture, real movement

**Audio DNA:** Ambient city sounds | Natural footfall rhythm | No score or emotional music | Walt's dry, charming delivery as centerpiece

**Human Performance DNA:** Calm, steady, unfazed | Smooth stride, relaxed arms, slight smile | Deadpan micro-humor | Zero "acting" — he is simply being himself

### TRANSFERABLE CREATIVE PRINCIPLES
1. Let a real person simply *exist* on camera doing something physical.
2. Create inspiration through minimal intervention.
3. Maintain slow, steady pacing with very few cuts.
4. Keep camera movement parallel or front-facing at a consistent speed.
5. Tone = friendly, relaxed, dry humor.
6. Let the environment provide authenticity (traffic, fog, pedestrians).

### SCENE STRUCTURE MAP
- **Act 1 — EARLY MORNING RUN:** Quiet, honest
- **Act 2 — WALT'S WISDOM:** Direct, calm
- **Act 3 — EPILOGUE SHOT:** Distance, legacy
- **Act 4 — NIKE BUTTON:** Simple, declarative

**Pacing Curve:** 0–3s environment establishes authenticity | 3–7s Walt's presence becomes emotional anchor | 7–11s Walt delivers message mid-run | 11–14s wide passing shot communicates longevity | 14–15s simple title card

### CAMERA/LENS/LIGHTING MAP
- 35mm parallel tracking authenticity | 50mm portrait realism | 24mm environmental scale | 35mm low-angle heroic minimalism
- Lighting: Always natural | Soft warm sunrise → crisp daylight | No artificial fill
- Movement: Always smooth, steady | Camera rhythm matches jogging cadence

### SAFETY ABSTRACTION
**DO NOT replicate:** Real Walt Stack's likeness, voice, personal identity, quotes, or exact historical events.
**ALLOWED:** Structural filmmaking principles | Pacing, tone, cadence, understated authenticity | "Real human doing something real" storytelling | Minimalist documentary DNA

## AD_KNOWLEDGE_PACKAGE: COCA_COLA_MEAN_JOE_GREENE

**TITLE:** "Hey Kid, Catch!"
**BRAND:** Coca-Cola | **YEAR:** 1980 | **DIRECTOR:** Lee Lacy
**CATEGORY:** Emotional · Human · Micro-Drama
**TONE:** Warm · Honest · Vulnerable → Heartfelt
**NARRATIVE DEVICE:** A wounded giant softens after a small act of kindness.
**WHY ICONIC:** One of the purest examples of emotional payoff in advertising; perfect empathy arc in under 60 seconds.

### MASTER CREATIVE DNA
**Core Themes:** Generosity between strangers | Unexpected softness from a tough figure | The power of a small gesture | Emotional reversal (tough → grateful) | Innocence meeting vulnerability

**Visual DNA:** Soft warm tunnel lighting (practicals) | Low-key shadows | Slight haze to emphasize heat/exhaustion | Close proximity shots for intimacy | Ground-level perspective to emphasize Joe's size

**Audio DNA:** Stadium ambience echoing faintly | Footsteps on concrete | Soft reverb in tunnel | Quiet, hesitant vocal performances | Minimal score; moment-driven soundscape

**Human Performance DNA — Mean Joe:** Exhausted, limping, slightly irritable | Slow, heavy body language | Emotional shift must be gradual and micro-expressive: tension → hesitation → softness → genuine warmth

**Human Performance DNA — The Kid:** Nervous admiration | Gentle vocal tone | Small physical presence vs Joe's imposing frame | Earnest without melodrama

### TRANSFERABLE CREATIVE PRINCIPLES
1. Use contrasting character archetypes (giant/tough vs small/hopeful).
2. Build emotional reversal through micro-performance.
3. Keep camera close and low for warmth and vulnerability.
4. Allow silence to do heavy lifting.
5. Let the "gift" moment symbolize connection.
6. Resolution must be warm, quiet, and deeply earned.

### SCENE STRUCTURE MAP
- **Act 1 — FATIGUE:** Joe limps down tunnel, armor removed, humanity visible.
- **Act 2 — THE OFFER:** Kid tries to help; Joe resists; tension softens.
- **Act 3 — THE TURN:** Joe accepts the drink; emotional shift begins.
- **Act 4 — THE RETURN GESTURE:** Joe tosses jersey; kid lights up; emotional payoff lands.
- **Act 5 — RESOLUTION:** Tunnel empties; warmth lingers.

**Pacing Curve:** 0–3s fatigue + silence | 3–6s kid appears | 6–10s offer + awkward hesitation | 10–14s acceptance → emotional shift | 14–18s payoff gesture | 18–20s quiet close

### CAMERA/LENS/LIGHTING MAP
- 35mm fatigue realism | 50mm earnest confrontation | 40mm two-shot emotional tension | 85mm emotional breakthrough | 28mm warmth and payoff
- Color Temp: 3000–3400K warm, intimate | No cold light ever used
- Movement: Slow dolly-back from large figure → stillness + gentle dolly-in for tension → dolly-out for emotional lift

### SAFETY ABSTRACTION
**DO NOT replicate:** Mean Joe Greene's real likeness, voice, jersey number, team, or historical performance.
**ALLOWED:** Emotional arc | Cinematic structure | Character archetypes (wounded giant + empathetic youth) | Warm tunnel lighting and pacing principles | Generosity-based storytelling

## AD_KNOWLEDGE_PACKAGE: WENDYS_WHERES_THE_BEEF

**TITLE:** "Where's The Beef?"
**BRAND:** Wendy's | **YEAR:** 1984 | **DIRECTOR:** Joe Sedelmaier
**CATEGORY:** Humor · Exaggeration · Cultural Catchphrase
**TONE:** Snarky · Observational · Exaggerated Realism
**NARRATIVE DEVICE:** Older women inspecting a comically oversized competitor bun to highlight tiny meat portions.
**WHY ICONIC:** Perfect comedic escalation + unforgettable catchphrase anchoring Wendy's "bigger, better beef" claim.

### MASTER CREATIVE DNA
**Core Themes:** Exposing competitors' shortcomings | Humor through exaggeration | Straight-faced delivery of absurdity | Punchline-driven escalation | Visual simplicity reinforcing message clarity

**Visual DNA:** Bright overhead fast-food lighting (flat, slightly unflattering) | Comically oversized bun dominating the frame | Sparse background | 1980s "consumer report" aesthetic | Tight table-level framings

**Audio DNA:** Slight cafeteria reverb | Muted ambient restaurant noise | Quiet buildup → loud punchline delivery

**Human Performance DNA — Lead Woman (Clara):** Small in size but large in personality | Perpetually unimpressed | Snarky, assertive, deadpan comic timing

**Human Performance DNA — Companions:** Mild confusion | Observational humor via reactions | Straight-faced "normal person" responses

### TRANSFERABLE CREATIVE PRINCIPLES
1. Use object exaggeration to reveal product truth.
2. Surround the absurd object with straight-faced characters.
3. Build toward a punchline with rhythmic dialogue beats.
4. Keep camera simple, intimate, table-level.
5. Use deadpan humor + escalating frustration.
6. Let props be the comedy engine.

### SCENE STRUCTURE MAP
- **Act 1 — DISCOVERY:** Older women examine huge bun + tiny patty.
- **Act 2 — CONFUSION:** Reaction shots, skeptical commentary.
- **Act 3 — ESCALATION:** Clara becomes annoyed; comedic tension rises.
- **Act 4 — PUNCHLINE:** Clara leans in, delivers the line.
- **Act 5 — BUTTON:** Additional reaction emphasizing absurdity.

**Pacing Curve:** 0–2s object reveal | 2–5s women react with confusion | 5–8s comedic escalation | 8–10s punchline delivery | 10–12s deadpan resolution

**Timing Rules:** Build punchline through 3 beats of confusion | Deliver punchline on beat 4 | Hold silence for 0.5s after punchline

### CAMERA/LENS/LIGHTING MAP
- 35mm table-level reveal | 50mm ensemble reactions | 85mm comedic close-up | 40mm escalation two-shot | 28mm comedic button wide
- Lighting: Always flat, overhead, bright | 4000K throughout | No dramatic shadows (flat = funnier)
- Movement: Mostly static | Occasional slow push-ins for escalation | Micro push for punchline emphasis

### SAFETY ABSTRACTION
**DO NOT replicate:** Real Clara Peller's likeness or voice | Exact bun design or dialogue beyond structural parody.
**ALLOWED:** Exaggerated object humor | Deadpan comedic pacing | "Tiny core product vs oversized competitor" storytelling | Table-level reaction shot structure | Escalation → punchline → reaction rhythm

## AD_KNOWLEDGE_PACKAGE: BUDWEISER_WHASSUP

**TITLE:** "Whassup?"
**BRAND:** Budweiser | **YEAR:** 1999 | **DIRECTOR:** Charles Stone III
**CATEGORY:** Dialogue Driven · Catchphrase · Ensemble Humor
**TONE:** Simple · Slacker · Communal Joy
**NARRATIVE DEVICE:** A simple, repetitive, drawn-out verbal greeting becomes a pervasive ritual among friends watching TV.
**WHY ICONIC:** Created a global, infectious catchphrase/meme; distilled casual friendship and communal experience into a simple, repetitive ritual.

### MASTER CREATIVE DNA
**Core Themes:** Simple, accessible friendship | Communal, effortless bonding (shared TV time) | The power of repetitive, absurd language | Product as the simple tool for connection

**Visual DNA:** Simple, low-key domestic settings (living rooms) | Warm, dim lighting (3000K) from television screen | Focus on actors' faces capturing genuine slightly ridiculous joy | Simple, static framing relying entirely on dialogue and performance

**Audio DNA:** Core sound is the drawn-out, repeated, rhythmic verbal greeting | Dialogue is minimal | Amplified background sound of television, phone noises, genuine communal laughter | No music — the rhythm IS the repetitive dialogue

**Human Performance DNA:** Must convey genuine, unforced camaraderie and delight | The repetition must escalate in volume and intensity | Expressions: simple, wide-eyed, slightly goofy smiles

### TRANSFERABLE CREATIVE PRINCIPLES
1. Build an entire sequence around a single, absurd, repetitive verbal cue.
2. Scene must be simple and intimate, featuring a core group of authentic-feeling friends.
3. The greeting must be performed as a chain reaction, passing the cue from person to person.
4. Product should be subtly integrated as the central object of shared downtime.
5. Humor comes from high commitment to low-stakes, repetitive absurdity.

### SCENE STRUCTURE MAP
- **Act 1 — THE INITIATION:** One friend starts the call/greeting.
- **Act 2 — THE REPETITION:** Chain reaction begins, passing phrase between friends.
- **Act 3 — THE CRESCENDO:** Group performs the phrase together in loud, joyous chorus.
- **Act 4 — THE RESOLUTION:** Friends relax, smiling, enjoying the moment.

**Pacing Curve:** 0–4s greeting initiated slowly | 4–8s repetition accelerates to high-volume peak | 8–10s collective, loud cheer | 10–12s return to quiet intimacy

### CAMERA/LENS/LIGHTING MAP
- 50mm intimate conversational close-ups | 85mm isolate faces for laughter/joy | 35mm wide to establish warm low-key setting
- Lighting: Warm 3000–3200K sourced primarily from off-screen television
- Movement: Mostly static, relying on quick rhythmic cutting between actors

### SAFETY ABSTRACTION
**DO NOT replicate:** Specific dialogue ("Whassup"), actors' likenesses, or specific product use.
**ALLOWED:** Simple Shared Ritual → Rhythmic Repetitive Dialogue → Communal Joy/Chain Reaction | Warm low-key domestic lighting | Reliance on single absurd verbal cue to drive the plot | Theme of simple, non-intellectual fun and easy bonding

## AD_KNOWLEDGE_PACKAGE: METRO_TRAINS_DUMB_WAYS_TO_DIE

**TITLE:** "Dumb Ways to Die"
**BRAND:** Metro Trains (Melbourne) | **YEAR:** 2012
**CATEGORY:** Animated · Public Service Announcement · Musical
**TONE:** Morbid · Catchy · Dark Humor
**NARRATIVE DEVICE:** Using cute, innocent characters to demonstrate extreme, preventable forms of death, culminating in train safety.
**WHY ICONIC:** Successfully rebranded a serious safety message using absurd, viral humor and a highly memorable song.

### MASTER CREATIVE DNA
**Core Themes:** Preventable absurdity | Contrast of innocence and consequence | Catchy musical repetition | Grouping disparate dangers to highlight the final, critical one

**Visual DNA:** Highly stylized, simple, pastel-colored 2D animation | Soft, rounded character designs | Bright, friendly color palette contrasting with outcomes | Fast, rhythmic cuts synced to musical beat

**Audio DNA:** Upbeat, acoustic, deceptively cheerful indie-pop score | Lyrics dark but delivered with light, airy voice | Cartoonish exaggerated sound effects | Repetitive, highly memorable chorus

**Human Performance DNA (Animation):** Simple movement | Exaggerated reaction to physical trauma while maintaining high level of cuteness

### TRANSFERABLE CREATIVE PRINCIPLES
1. Use extreme juxtaposition (cute animation + dark subject matter) to create memorability.
2. Employ a musical hook that is deceptively cheerful to carry a heavy message.
3. Structure the narrative as a list, building rhythm and momentum.
4. Reserve the main message (the most serious danger) for the final, sudden transition.
5. Use simple, non-offensive visuals for complex, offensive concepts.

### SCENE STRUCTURE MAP
- **Act 1 — THE WACKY DEATHS:** Building the humorous list
- **Act 2 — THE CHORUS:** Musical, rhythmic refrain
- **Act 3 — THE RAIL DEATHS:** Serious final warnings
- **Act 4 — RESOLUTION:** Final safety message

**Pacing Curve:** 0–5s quick rhythmic introduction | 5–8s first chorus | 8–15s continued list | 15–18s second chorus | 18–22s sudden shift to rail-specific danger | 22–25s final graphic button

### CAMERA/LENS/LIGHTING MAP
- 35mm/50mm static shots for comedic isolation | Low-angle train shot (35mm) | Wide 24mm for final safe resolution
- Color Temp: Bright friendly 6000–6500K | Quick flashes of red for dangerous moments | Slightly cooler 4000K for train scene
- Movement: Extremely rapid cuts synced to song's beat | Song drives the rhythm, not the camera

### SAFETY ABSTRACTION
**DO NOT replicate:** Specific animated characters, the song, or exact lyric structure.
**ALLOWED:** Cute characters for dark humor | List structure to build to serious climax | Contrasting cheerful music with grim visuals | Final sudden shift in tone to deliver core message

**NARRATIVE DEVICE:** A man's perfect life is ruined by a single, common craving (milk) and a moment of historical trivia failure.
**WHY ICONIC:** Defined a successful, question-based tagline; popularized the concept of an entire narrative built around a single, specific food craving.

**Core Themes:** The agony of simple frustration | The power of food cravings | Escalating situational doom | Absurd consequences for minor failures

**Human Performance DNA:** Protagonist starts relaxed, then descends rapidly into manic, aggressive frustration | Emphasis on physical actions: ripping boxes, clawing at throat, frantic searching | Face must convey physical pain of dry mouth/throat

### TRANSFERABLE CREATIVE PRINCIPLES
1. Establish a moment of perfect, desired food consumption.
2. Introduce a single, missing ingredient (the product) that stops the consumption.
3. Rapidly escalate the character's frustration through manic physical action and dialogue.
4. Use the tension and pacing to make the final reveal/punchline feel like a catastrophic failure.
5. Utilize intense sound design (crunching, swallowing, dry mouth sounds) to make the physical discomfort palpable.

### SCENE STRUCTURE MAP
- **Act 1 — THE PERFECTION:** The desire is achieved
- **Act 2 — THE ABSENCE:** Product is gone/empty
- **Act 3 — THE ESCALATION:** Raging search/frustration
- **Act 4 — THE FINAL CHALLENGE:** The trivia question
- **Act 5 — THE CLIMAX:** Failure and devastation

### CAMERA/LENS/LIGHTING MAP
- 85mm intimacy and initial satisfaction/final defeat | 35mm handheld manic frustration | 24mm wide reveal of total situational doom
- Color Temp: Dominantly 3400K warm domestic — emphasizes the comfort that is lost
- Movement: Sharp shift from smooth/static to manic/handheld | Camera movement mimics character's internal panic | Final slow agonizing push-in on defeat

### SAFETY ABSTRACTION
**DO NOT replicate:** Specific historical figures (Aaron Burr, Alexander Hamilton), exact dialogue, or physical likeness of protagonist.
**ALLOWED:** Perfect Food Moment → Missing Ingredient → Catastrophic Consequences narrative | Manic energy and fast cuts to convey desperation | Comedic principle of high stakes for a low-stakes problem | Powerful sound design to convey physical discomfort

### SAFETY ABSTRACTION

## CROSS-CUTTING CREATIVE PRINCIPLES

These patterns appear across multiple iconic ads and represent the most transferable creative strategies:

### Emotional Architecture
- **Wounded Giant + Empathetic Youth** (Mean Joe Greene) — vulnerability from strength creates deep emotional resonance
- **Fantasy Effort → Mundane Failure → Surprise Success** (VW The Force) — earned payoff via honest setup
- **Chronological Bond Over Time** (Farmer's Dog) — time as the ultimate emotional currency

### Humor Mechanics
- **Deadpan + Escalation** (Wendy's, Got Milk) — straight-faced delivery of absurdity always lands
- **Visual/Audio Mismatch** (E*Trade Baby) — incongruity between what you see and what you hear
- **Repetitive Ritual** (Budweiser Whassup) — commitment to a single absurd bit signals confidence
- **Self-Aware Celebrity** (Arnold, Jason Momoa) — stars who mock themselves are instantly likable
- **Punchline Timing Rule** — build through 3 beats of confusion, deliver on beat 4, hold silence 0.5s

### Production Principles
- **Single Continuous Shot** (Old Spice) — the technical constraint creates the creative constraint
- **Real Human Just Existing** (Nike Walt Stack) — authenticity beats production value every time
- **Low Budget + High Absurdity** (Dollar Shave Club) — the mismatch IS the message
- **Sound as the Ad** (Alka-Seltzer, McDonald's) — the sonic logo is more durable than any visual

### Structural Archetypes
- **The Founder Walk-and-Talk** (Dollar Shave Club) — personal, energetic, anti-corporate
- **The Binary Debate** (Miller Lite) — simple, eternal, never resolved
- **The Comparative Archetype** (Apple Mac vs PC) — personify the brands, let them talk
- **The Taboo-with-Elegance** (Poo-Pourri, Squatty Potty) — own the embarrassing thing first
